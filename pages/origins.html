Origins

The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are 17 global objectives established by UN member states in 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda to eliminate poverty, protect the planet, and ensure peace and prosperity for all. These goals succeeded the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) and consist of specific targets to measure progress.

The creation of the SDGs involved extensive consultations with various stakeholders and culminated in their adoption following the Rio+20 conference in 2012. The goals address challenges such as poverty, inequality, climate change, environmental degradation, peace, and justice.
